--- label: 3.1 title: What Impact Do They Have on Local Communities? --- label: 3.1.1 title: Utility Costs content: Citizens who live near data centers are reporting increases in their energy and water bills. This is no coincidence. As we now know, data centers are big energy consumers. The impacts of these demands are being felt by residents in the surrounding areas. Electricity, for example, comes mainly from state and regional grids, and increases in demand result in higher prices for residents and businesses alike. This means that everyone from a household, to a local small business, to data centers see increases in prices. But the difference is that the massive companies funding the data centers can afford these increases. Citizens might not be able to. This graph shows the increases in prices for electricity, gas, and water for residential properties. Each has increased at a rate higher than inflation since 2020 to 2025. Residents living near data centers say that their bills have increased by far more than the national average price increase shown above. --- label: 3.1.2 title: Pollution content: Since data centers require so much energy, they directly contribute to an increase in the amounts of fossil fuels we burn every day. This is the most direct driving factor behind global warming and climate change. The mining of certain metals needed for creating chips. Mining these metals also leads to environmental destruction. Data centers are only getting hungrier and hungrier for more power, meaning that without widespread adoption of cleaner energy sources, these data centers will accelerate the speed at which harmful greenhouse gases are released into the atmosphere. --- label: 3.1.3 title: Job Loss content: AI will lead to a massive increase in unemployment. No human can compete with the efficiency and output of AI. We are already seeing the effects of job loss due to AI, and this problem will only get worse with more time. AI poses a serious threat to jobs of all kinds, including both blue collar and white collar work. --- label: 3.2.1 title: What Are the Key Impacts of Data Centers on Their Surroundings? content: By virtue of using up so much electricity and water, there is much less of these resources for surrounding communities. The construction of a data center can suddenly raise energy prices in nearby communities by as much as 20% because it becomes a much more scarce resource. The water shortage has a major impact on residents too, as some people have even resorted to drinking recycled wastewater (sewage water treated at a facility). --- label: 3.2.2 title: What Do People Who Live Near These Data Centers Have to Say? content: Despite understanding their functioning and contribution to modern society, those who live near data centers are not happy. Their most common complaint is the constant noise produced by the cooling systems and vents. In some extreme cases, living close enough to this noise is enough to vibrate your home! In 2023, support for data centers amongst residents of Virginia was at 69%. Today, that number is down to just 35%, and decreasing. --- label: 3.2.3 title: Health Hazards Posed by Data Centers content: Air pollution and contamination caused by data centers is already being linked to a number of health problems. The fossil fuels they burn for power, often on site by backup generators, cause cardiovascular and respiratory issues. Unclean water resulting from contamination also leads to health issues both through impure drinking water and impurities in water needed for hygiene and sanitation. These are all health hazards, and are major concerns for local residents in areas where data centers are planned or already exist. ---
