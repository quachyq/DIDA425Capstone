--- label: 1.1 title: What is a Data Center? --- label: 1.1.1 title: Definition content: At its simplest, a data center is just a building that houses technology infrastructure such as servers, data storage systems, networking devices, and operating systems. Previously, companies would own a data center to facilitate their computing needs. Nowadays, cloud service providers (CSPs) own massive data centers capable of fulfilling the needs for multiple separate companies at once. --- label: 1.2 title: Bones of a Data Center --- label: 1.2.1 title: What Are Servers, Racks, and Networking Equipment? content: The “technology infrastructure” are the physical pieces of equipment housed by the data center. The main components are the following:
  • Data storage arrays
    • As the name suggests, these store user and company data. Storing data through these rather than getting it from the user’s computer saves time.
  • Web servers
    • These are the main computational components. These run software and do any necessary computations for the programs running.
  • Power sources
    • Large generators and batteries are used to keep the system running even during power outages.
  • Cooling equipment
    • Usually either giant fans or water cooling systems are used to keep the rest of the equipment from overheating.
--- label: 1.3 title: What Are They Used For? --- label: 1.3.1 title: What Services Depend On Them? content: Data centers are an essential part of almost all modern day digital systems. Everything from AI, to Facebook, to Netflix use data centers in order to store and process data. Modern data centers are also capable of providing computing power, meaning that the burden of computation is taken away from individual devices, allowing users to access complex platforms on all kinds of devices. In the past, companies would have to build their own data centers. Now, large data centers can provide rentable space for a number of companies at once. --- label: 1.3.2 title: What is the Difference in Data Center Usage Between a Company like Netflix vs. OpenAI? content: Services like Netflix use data centers to deliver already existing content to users. These systems are highly efficient, and are considered to be low power systems. Services such as OpenAI’s ChatGPT, however, use a lot more power and resources because they are generative. ChatGPT and other large language models such as Google Gemini, Meta AI, and others do not deliver existing data files, they are used to create new content. Many of these services now offer image and or video creation. This requires the model to use lots of energy. In 2024, data centers accounted for about 4.4% of total electricity consumption in the US, and that number is expected to grow to anywhere from 6.7 to 12% by 2028. --- label: 1.4 title: What is the Scope of the Issue? --- label: 1.4.1 title: How Many Data Centers Are There Now? content: The US currently has about 5,400 active facilities. The biggest hubs are in Northern Virginia, Chicago, New Jersey, New York, Los Angeles, and Miami. Another ~3,000 are planned or currently under construction. --- label: 1.4.2 title: How Many More Will There Be In 10 Years? content: We expect that by 2030, data centers will more than triple their total energy consumption across the country from 2024. This means that they would account for 13% of all energy consumption in the United States! --- label: 1.4.3 title: Where Are They Being Built? content: Currently, in the US, there are the most data centers in Virginia and Texas, and that’s exactly where plans to build more are. Existing plans for new data centers will double the number of data centers in each of these states. Looking towards the rest of the US, Georgia, Pennsylvania, Ohio, New Mexico, and Illinois are key states to keep an eye on for future data center development. --- label: 1.5 title: Who Profits From Them? --- label: 1.5.1 title: Large Corporations content: Companies have two main revenue streams regarding data centers. Firstly, in physical chips and hardware. Companies like Nvidia and Broadcom generate immense amounts of revenue. These companies made about $194 billion and $20 billion last year, respectively. The other avenue for profit is from cloud services, which is software. Companies such as Amazon Web Services and Microsoft made $129 billion and $106 billion in revenue in 2025. --- label: 1.5.2 title: Costs vs. Profits content: It costs a lot of money to build and maintain a data center. It can cost around tens of millions of dollars just to build the infrastructure for a large data center. The cost depends on size and location. In order to maintain the data center, operating costs are around $10-25 million annually. However, data centers are highly profitable, and the global data center market made approximately $350 billion in 2024, and that amount is expected to nearly double by 2029. This means that data centers are big investments, however they are immensely profitable for companies that can afford to build and then rent them. --- label: 1.6 title: Why Should We Care? --- label: 1.6.1 title: Who Are The Stakeholders? content: Many data centers are built with the purpose of running one or a few projects. For example, an entire data center might be used to process ChatGPT prompts. While this means the construction of a data center can affect practically anyone (since things like ChatGPT are public to use), its most noticeable effects are on the following groups:
  • The companies that use the data center to run their computing software. Data centers exist to streamline these processes, helping companies run more efficiently and save money.
  • Locals. While it may not often be thought about, those who live near data centers put up with some major negative consequences, such as the strong noise pollution from all the machines running at the center 24/7.
--- label: 1.6.2 title: What Are the Negative Impacts? content: The main negative impacts for locals are mostly the noise that the data center causes, sometimes even making it difficult for people to sleep, even when measures are taken to make the data center quieter. However, the truly large-scale effects come into play with how energy and water-intensive running a data center is. Even a medium-sized data center can use as much power as 100,000 homes, and really drain nearby water sources. This is the main cause of concern for scaling them up across the country. ---
